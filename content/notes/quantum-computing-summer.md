Summer of Science is IIT Bombay's reading programme: pick a subject, a mentor, and eight weeks. I picked quantum computing with the Maths and Physics Club.

The first half was quantum mechanics proper: state vectors, unitaries, measurement, and why entanglement is not merely correlation. The second half was practical. I simulated quantum logic gates and circuits in Qiskit, then worked through the standard algorithms, Deutsch–Jozsa, Grover, and the quantum Fourier transform behind Shor, to see where the speed-up actually comes from and where noise takes it away.

I also reviewed reports from the programme and read through industry and research work to understand what was engineering and what was still physics. The honest conclusion at the time: the interesting work for someone like me was in the classical layers around the machine, compilation, error mitigation, and simulation, rather than in the qubits themselves.
