A demo is useful. It creates momentum, compresses an idea into something tangible, and gives a team a shared object to discuss. But a demo is weak evidence. It usually hides the retries, hand-selected inputs, human interventions, and failures that shaped the final path.

For AI systems, that gap matters. The output can look convincing while the route to it remains unstable. A reviewer needs more than the polished result: the question, the hypothesis, the tools used, the observations that changed the approach, and the boundary where confidence ends.

## A better unit of proof

The useful artifact is not a longer demo. It is a compact chain from claim to evidence. Each link should answer a practical question:

- What were we trying to learn?
- What result would have changed our mind?
- Which failures survived the edit?
- Can another person reproduce the conclusion?

This does not require a platform. Plain records, stable identifiers, and direct links to artifacts are often enough. The discipline matters more than the interface.

## Design consequence

That is why this portfolio is structured like a small annual report. The visual layer earns attention; the report structure spends that attention on judgment. Each finished case study will distinguish verified outcomes from working hypotheses and keep failure close to the result.
